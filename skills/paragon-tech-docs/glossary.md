# Paragon editorial glossary (Tone of Voice)

## Overview

Editorial conventions from Paragon Tone of Voice for technical and other written Markdown. Follow these unless the `paragon-tech-docs` skill documents a technical-documentation exception.

Source: Paragon Tone of Voice guide (glossary + general writing tips). Marketing vision/proposition pages are out of scope here.

## Writing tips (must follow)

1. **Active voice** — direct and clear. Avoid distant passive corporate wording.
2. **Confidence** — state what you know. Avoid empty hedging (*perhaps*, *maybe*) unless uncertainty is the point. If unsure of a fact, omit it.
3. **Warm, human language** — use contractions (*we're*, *you're*, *won't*). Prefer *colleagues* or *people* over cold *employees* / *staff* where natural.
4. **First person for Paragon** — use *we* / *our* when speaking as the organisation; keep the subject clear (product, team, or system when precision needs it). Do not narrate as third-person "Paragon enables…".
5. **No buzzword filler** — avoid or replace: 360 service/view, best-in-class, continuous improvement, cutting-edge, experienced (as empty filler), physical (as vague filler), going forward / moving forward, innovative (when generic), journey to, market leading, unprecedented, one-stop shop, unique (when generic), value add.
6. **Show, don't tell** — back claims with specifics (scale, scope, standards). Avoid stacking empty adjectives (*perfect*, *unique*, *dynamic*).
7. **Everyday language** — prefer short words (extra not additional; before not prior to; make sure not ensure; while not whilst; and so on).

## Glossary A–Y

### A
- **Abbreviations:** Do not use full stops after abbreviations such as Ltd, Inc, Mr, Mrs, Dr. Use full stops correctly in Latin abbreviations (for example *i.e.*, *e.g.*, *etc.*).
- **Acronyms:** Spell out on first use, then acronym in brackets — for example direct mail (DM).
- **Ampersands:** Do not use `&` unless part of a brand/company name (for example M&S), or where *and* would make a multi-part list unclear.

### B
- **Brackets:** If a whole sentence is in brackets, keep the full stop inside. Otherwise leave the full stop outside.
- **Bold:** Highlight sparingly or emphasis is diluted. In technical docs, bold is for UI labels (see skill).

### C
- **Capital letters:** Avoid BLOCK CAPITALS. Capitals for proper nouns. Headings: sentence case (first letter only), matching the skill.
- **Colons and semicolons:** Colons introduce a list or a whole quote. Semicolons mark a longer pause than a comma.

### D
- **Dates:** UK format — for example Wednesday 21 December 2022 or 21/12/22.

### E
- **Exclamation marks:** Very sparingly; never more than one.

### F
- **Full stops:** End of sentences. Do not use full stops after short fragment bullet items in brand-style lists. **Technical exception:** numbered procedure steps that are full sentences may use full stops (see skill).

### G
- **Gender-inclusive:** Prefer spokesperson, chair/chairperson, humankind, salesperson.
- **Global:** Prefer *international* over *global* for Paragon footprint wording.

### H
- **Hyphens:** Hyphenate compound adjectives before a noun (*round-the-clock support*). No hyphen when the phrase follows the noun (*support round the clock*).

### I
- **Italics:** Sparingly — publication names, or rare emphasis on a word/phrase.

### J
- **Jargon:** Remove buzzwords, clichés, and unexplained jargon. Prefer straightforward language.

### K
- **Kilograms / kilometres:** *kg* and *km*.

### L
- **LinkedIn:** Personal posts reflect on Paragon; stay professional and consistent with this guide.
- **Lists in prose:** Do not list more than three items in a sentence separated by commas. For longer sets use bullets, a table, or a numbered procedure. **Technical exception:** procedures may have more than three numbered steps.
- **Language:** Personal style is fine if consistent and still follows these conventions.

### M
- **Money:** Use symbols (£100, $100, €100). Decimals only when needed (£100.50, not £100.00).

### N
- **Numbers:** Spell out one to nine in sentences; use numerals from 10. Ordinals: spell first to twentieth, then 21st…. Thousands use commas (1,000). Prefer decimals for fractions (3.5).

### O
- **Online:** One word. **OK:** write *OK* (not *ok* or *okay*).

### P
- **Percentages:** Use `%`. In a range, only at the end (30-40%).
- **Presentation writing:** Limit text per slide; split dense slides; use notes when presenting.
- **Press releases:** Do not issue your own. Route through central communications.

### Q
- **Quotes:** Introduce with a colon. Double quotation marks for the quote; single for a quote within a quote. Full stop inside if the quote is a full sentence; outside if the quote is part of a larger sentence.

### R
- **Regions:** Common acronyms (UK, US). Upper case for named regions (North America); lower case for non-specific (*the north of England*).

### S
- **Spelling:** Always spellcheck; use **UK** spellings (check Office defaults). Proof important documents.
- **Storytelling:** Prefer real examples over abstract claims when it helps the reader.
- **Sweeping statements:** Back big claims with evidence or omit them.

### T
- **Testimonials:** Need client permission before external use; quotation marks; name and company after the quote.

### U
- **United Kingdom / spelling:** UK English, not US.

### V
- **Voice:** Active, not passive.

### W
- **Websites:** Include `www.` when writing Paragon web addresses (for example www.paragon.world).

### Y
- **Years:** Financial years as 2022/23; year ranges as 2010-11.
